# MTG Formats & Legality
> Focused on MTG Arena (MTGA). Tabletop differences noted where relevant.
> Sources: Wizards of the Coast (magic.wizards.com), Scryfall. Current as of May 2026.
> Note: Format legality and rotation dates are live data - verify current state via Scryfall.

---

## Overview

A **format** defines which cards are legal in a deck and what rules apply. Different formats have different card pools, deck sizes, and rotation schedules. MTGA supports several formats simultaneously.

**Two main categories:**
- **Constructed** - You build a deck from your collection ahead of time.
- **Limited** - You build a deck on the spot from randomly opened packs (Draft or Sealed).

---

## MTGA Constructed Formats

### Standard (Rotating)
- **What it is:** The flagship competitive format. Uses only the most recently released sets.
- **Deck size:** 60 cards minimum, up to 15-card sideboard (Bo3 only).
- **Copy limit:** Max 4 copies of any card (except basic lands).
- **Life total:** 20.
- **Rotation:** Once per year (historically Fall). The oldest year of sets rotates out. Sets are legal for approximately 2-3 years.
- **Ban list:** Yes. Cards are occasionally banned for format health.
- **Best for:** Competitive play, staying current with new sets, lower collection investment than eternal formats.

> **Tabletop difference:** Physical Standard rotates on the same schedule but takes effect the Friday after the Arena patch.

---

### Alchemy (Rotating, Digital-Only)
- **What it is:** A digital-only format based on Standard, but with Arena-exclusive cards and rebalanced versions of existing cards.
- **Deck size:** 60 cards minimum, up to 15-card sideboard.
- **Rotation:** Every two years (faster than Standard).
- **Key difference from Standard:** Includes digital-only keywords (Seek, Conjure, Perpetually, Spellbook). Cards marked "A-" are Alchemy-rebalanced versions. No ban list - problem cards are nerfed (rebalanced) instead.
- **Card pool:** Current Standard sets + Alchemy-exclusive sets.
- **Best for:** Players who want Standard-adjacent play with Arena-specific design space.

> **Note:** Alchemy cards are NOT legal in Standard. Standard cards ARE legal in Alchemy.

---

### Pioneer (Non-Rotating)
- **What it is:** A non-rotating format using sets from *Return to Ravnica* (2012) onward. Mirrors the tabletop Pioneer format exactly.
- **Deck size:** 60 cards minimum, up to 15-card sideboard.
- **Card pool:** All sets from Return to Ravnica forward. No Alchemy or digital-only cards.
- **Ban list:** Yes, mirrors the tabletop Pioneer ban list.
- **History on MTGA:** Was previously called "Explorer" on Arena. Explorer was retired and fully replaced by Pioneer in May 2025 after all relevant cards were added.
- **Best for:** Players who also play tabletop and want parity between digital and paper.

---

### Historic (Non-Rotating)
- **What it is:** A non-rotating format with nearly every card ever released on MTGA, including Alchemy cards.
- **Deck size:** 60 cards minimum, up to 15-card sideboard.
- **Card pool:** All Standard and Alchemy sets, Historic Anthologies, remastered sets, bonus sheet cards, and other supplemental Arena releases.
- **Ban list:** Yes, has its own ban list separate from other formats.
- **Key note:** Includes Alchemy-rebalanced versions of cards.
- **Best for:** Veteran Arena players with large collections who want access to powerful older cards.

---

### Timeless (Non-Rotating, Highest Power)
- **What it is:** Arena's most powerful constructed format. Every card on Arena is legal in its original, unrebalanced form.
- **Deck size:** 60 cards minimum, up to 15-card sideboard.
- **Card pool:** Every card on MTGA. Alchemy cards legal. Paper cards use original (not rebalanced) versions.
- **Restriction list:** Uses a Restricted List instead of bans. Restricted cards are limited to 1 copy per deck.
- **No ban list** - instead, problematic cards are restricted to 1 copy.
- **Best for:** Experienced players who want access to the most powerful cards and combos on Arena.

> **Tabletop parallel:** Most similar to Vintage - anything goes with a tiny restricted list.

---

### Brawl (Non-Rotating, Commander-Style)
- **What it is:** Arena's version of Commander. 1v1, 100-card singleton decks led by a Commander (any Legendary creature or Planeswalker on Arena).
- **Deck size:** 100 cards (Commander included). Singleton - only 1 copy of each card (except basic lands).
- **Commander:** Your chosen Legendary creature or Planeswalker sits in the Command Zone. Your deck's colors are determined by your Commander's color identity.
- **Life total:** 25 (not 20).
- **Sideboard:** None.
- **Mulligan:** One free mulligan.
- **Best of:** Bo1 only.
- **Best for:** Players who enjoy Commander-style deckbuilding and casual 1v1 games.

> **Tabletop difference:** Commander is 4-player. Brawl is strictly 1v1. Commander starts at 40 life; Brawl starts at 25.

---

### Standard Brawl (Rotating, Commander-Style)
- **What it is:** Same as Brawl, but restricted to Standard-legal cards only.
- **Deck size:** 60 cards (Commander included). Singleton.
- **Card pool:** Current Standard sets only.
- **Life total:** 25.
- **Best for:** New players who want Commander-style play without needing a massive collection.

---

## MTGA Limited Formats

### Draft (Premier Draft / Quick Draft)
- **What it is:** You pick cards one at a time from rotating packs, build a 40-card deck, and play.
- **Deck size:** 40 cards minimum (including lands you add).
- **Process:** 8 players each open a pack, pick 1 card, pass the pack. Repeat with 3 packs total.
- **Premier Draft:** Play against other humans. Best of 1 (7 wins or 3 losses ends the run).
- **Quick Draft:** Play against AI bots. Lower stakes, good for practicing a set.
- **Sealed:** Open 6 packs, build a 40-card deck from what you open. No drafting involved.
- **Best for:** Low collection requirement (you keep every card you draft), experiencing new sets.

---

## Rotation Explained

Rotation is when older sets leave a format, making their cards no longer legal.

**Standard rotation schedule:**
- Happens once per year (historically Fall).
- The oldest year of sets (4 sets) leaves simultaneously.
- Cards you own stay in your collection - they're just no longer legal in Standard.
- Rotating cards remain legal in Pioneer, Historic, Timeless, and Brawl forever.

**What happens to your cards after rotation (MTGA):**
- They don't disappear. You keep them.
- You can still play them in Historic, Pioneer, Timeless, and Brawl.

## Card Legality Rules

- **4-copy limit:** In all non-singleton constructed formats, you may have at most 4 copies of any card by name across your main deck and sideboard combined.
- **Basic land exception:** Basic lands have no copy limit.
- **Singleton formats** (Brawl) allow only 1 copy of each non-basic-land card.
- **Alchemy rebalancing:** In Historic and Brawl, if a paper card has been rebalanced by Alchemy, only the rebalanced version is available. The "A-" prefix identifies these cards.

## Banned & Restricted Lists

Wizards maintains ban/restriction lists at: **magic.wizards.com/en/game-info/gameplay/rules-and-formats/banned-restricted**

- **Banned** - Cannot be played in that format at all (0 copies).
- **Restricted** - Only 1 copy allowed per deck (used in Timeless only on Arena).
- Bans happen when a card creates unfun or oppressive play patterns.
- MTGA enforces all legality automatically.

## Color Identity (For Brawl/Commander)

Color identity determines which cards can go in your Brawl deck. It includes:
- All colored mana symbols in the card's mana cost.
- All colored mana symbols in the card's rules text (including reminder text).
- Both faces of a double-faced card.

Example: A Commander with color identity {W}{U} (White-Blue) means your entire 99-card deck can only contain White, Blue, and colorless cards.
