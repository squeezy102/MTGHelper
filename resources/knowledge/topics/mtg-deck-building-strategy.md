## The Basics: What Makes a Deck

Every 60-card constructed deck (Standard, Historic, Pioneer, etc.) needs:
1. A **win condition** - how you actually win the game.
2. **Threats** - cards that pressure the opponent toward your win condition.
3. **Answers** - cards that deal with the opponent's threats.
4. **Mana** - lands and/or ramp to cast everything.

Without a clear win condition, you're just playing individual good cards with no direction. Knowing how you win shapes every other decision.

## Deck Archetypes

An archetype is the broad category of strategy your deck follows. Most decks fit into one of four categories.

### Aggro
- **Goal:** Kill the opponent as fast as possible, typically by turn 4-5.
- **How:** Fill the deck with cheap, efficient creatures and direct damage. Apply pressure from turn 1.
- **Mana curve:** Very low. Mostly 1-3 mana cards. Usually 20-22 lands.
- **Strengths:** Consistent, punishing slow opponents, cheap to build.
- **Weaknesses:** Runs out of cards fast, loses to board wipes and lifegain.

### Control
- **Goal:** Survive the early game, answer every threat, then win with powerful late-game cards.
- **How:** Counterspells, removal, card draw. Trade resources efficiently until you can land an overwhelming finisher.
- **Mana curve:** Higher. Heavy on 2-4 mana interaction, 5+ mana finishers. Usually 26-28 lands.
- **Strengths:** Flexible, wins long games, great against creature-heavy decks.
- **Weaknesses:** Weak to early aggression, requires more skill to pilot.

### Midrange
- **Goal:** Play individually powerful cards that are stronger than the opponent's on a turn-by-turn basis.
- **How:** Mix of threats and answers. Good cards at every point on the curve.
- **Mana curve:** 2-5 mana range. Usually 23-25 lands.
- **Strengths:** Flexible, can play offense or defense depending on matchup.
- **Weaknesses:** Jack of all trades - specialists can outperform in specific matchups.

### Combo
- **Goal:** Assemble a specific combination of cards that wins instantly or creates an unbeatable advantage.
- **How:** Fill the deck with the combo pieces and cards that find/protect them.
- **Strengths:** Can win "out of nowhere," ignores board state.
- **Weaknesses:** Fragile - disrupting one piece can stop the whole plan.

### Ramp (Sub-Archetype)
- **Goal:** Accelerate mana production to deploy threats several turns ahead of schedule.
- **How:** Play mana-generating spells and creatures early, then spend that extra mana on powerful late-game cards.
- Often categorized as a type of Midrange or Control.

## Mana Curve

The mana curve is how many cards in your deck cost 1, 2, 3, 4+ mana.

**The goal:** Play something meaningful every turn. "Curving out" means playing a card on turn 1, a card on turn 2, etc. without wasted mana.

**Rough targets by archetype (60-card deck):**

| Archetype | 1-mana | 2-mana | 3-mana | 4-mana | 5+ mana | Lands |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aggro | 8-12 | 8-10 | 4-6 | 2-4 | 0-2 | 20-22 |
| Midrange | 4-6 | 6-10 | 8-10 | 4-6 | 2-4 | 23-25 |
| Control | 0-4 | 6-8 | 8-12 | 4-8 | 4-8 | 25-27 |

## The Mana Base (Land Selection)

### How Many Lands?
- **20-22:** Aggressive decks. You need to hit 2-3 mana reliably, not more.
- **23-25:** Midrange decks. You need 4-5 mana on time.
- **25-27:** Control decks. You need to hit land drops through turns 5-7.
- **36-38:** Brawl (100-card Commander-style). More cards, same land probability math.

### Color Fixing
- **Dual lands** produce two colors.
- **Taplands** enter tapped (cost you a turn of tempo) but produce multiple colors.
- **Shocklands** enter untapped if you pay 2 life. Faster but costs life.

## The Rule of 4

In most constructed formats, you can run up to 4 copies of any card.

- **4 copies:** Cards you always want to draw. Key threats, key payoffs, your best cards.
- **3 copies:** Cards you want most games but not always multiples of.
- **2 copies:** Situational cards, expensive finishers.
- **1 copy:** Tutoring targets, silver bullets for specific matchups.

## Sideboard (Bo3 Only)

A sideboard is up to 15 cards outside your main deck used to adapt between games in Best of 3.

- Address your main deck's weaknesses.
- Target the most common decks in the format.
- Know what to bring in AND what to take out for each matchup.
- Generally only run 2-3 copies of each sideboard card.

> **MTGA note:** Bo1 (Best of 1) is most common in ranked play on Arena. No actual card swaps occur in Bo1.

## Common Deckbuilding Pitfalls

- **Too many high-cost cards:** You'll do nothing for too many turns.
- **No clear win condition:** "Good cards" with no coherent plan rarely win consistently.
- **Bad mana base:** Playing the right spells doesn't matter if you can't cast them.
- **Too many situational cards:** Cards that are only good in specific scenarios dilute consistency.
- **Not enough lands in Brawl:** 36-38 lands for 100-card decks. New players routinely run too few.

## MTGA-Specific Tips

- **Wildcards are precious.** Use rare and mythic wildcards on cards you'll play across multiple decks or formats. Avoid spending on cards about to rotate out of Standard.
- **Check rotation dates** before crafting any rare or mythic for Standard.
- **Copy deck codes:** Decklists found online can be imported directly into MTGA via the deck builder's import function.
